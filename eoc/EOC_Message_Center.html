This EOC — who we are and who we report to

      
The same Message Center runs at every EOC. A Satellite EOC (in a ward building) covers part of a stake and rolls up to the Stake EOC. Above the Stake EOC the Church structure has two lines: the council line (the coordinating council, that is the Area Seventy: decisions) and the welfare line (the local Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager (WSRM), then the Area Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager (AWSRM): supplies, approvals, reporting). The SITREP goes to both; forms go to the welfare line. A Council / Area EOC exists only when the council or the welfare region opens one (the Area Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist's station); then the stakes report to it and their roll-ups merge there. The level sets the roster vocabulary (wards or stakes) and the SITREP header.

        Basemap for offline use
        
Save roads, terrain and aerial photos of your area on this laptop, so the Map tab and the wall show them with no internet. The maps come from the USGS (United States Geological Survey) The National Map.

        

        

        

        
Do it once, before an emergency, while this laptop has internet. Downloading tells USGS which area you asked for, and nothing else. Only the laptop that runs the EOC server can download. When a browser has internet, the Map tab can also show the online street map from OpenStreetMap.

      

      
Phones on the EOC Wi-Fi open forms from this server, and a plain-http page cannot read a phone's GPS, so those forms are filled in with the EOC location (the worker can change it). The Wi-Fi name and password put a "join the EOC Wi-Fi" QR code on the wall display, next to the "send reports" code; leave them blank to keep the password off the wall.

Roll-up to the EOC above us

      
Export a roll-up bundle (one .json file) and send it to the parent EOC with every SITREP: P / A attach it to an email to the parent EOC's drop email; C attach it to a Winlink message to the parent's Winlink address (keep "only new" ticked so it stays small); E carry it on a USB (Universal Serial Bus) drive with the printed SITREP. The parent's Drop Folder or Winlink watcher merges it automatically. Nothing is counted twice: the parent skips any record it already has, even one that also arrived by Winlink forward.

EMCOMM Comms Sheet — contacts, Winlink addresses, gateways and nets

      
One sheet for the whole stake, edited here by the stake Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist (emergency communications) and shown to everyone: the Comms tab on every operator's screen, the wall display, a printable one-page sheet for go-bags, a text block to paste into a Winlink message or email, and a small JSON file that any other EOC (Satellite, Stake Center or Area) takes on board automatically when it arrives by Drop Folder, Winlink attachment or USB — the newer sheet always wins. Points of contact and the Winlink addresses are for the chain field → Satellite EOC → Stake EOC → the council line (Area Seventy) and the welfare line (WSRM, then AWSRM). Gateways are the Winlink RMS (Radio Message Server) stations to try, in order, at Contingency (VHF/UHF, very high / ultra high frequency: VARA FM, then VARA HF). Voice nets are the stake amateur net and the ward FRS/GMRS (Family Radio Service / General Mobile Radio Service) channels. Lines for this event only (a net moved to simplex, a temporary gateway) sit on top and clear when the event closes.

      

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
      

      
Share it: Export sheet JSON → attach to an email to each EOC's drop email (P/A), to a Winlink message to each EOC's Winlink address (C, about 2 KB), or carry on USB (E) — the receiving EOC's server files it automatically; in standalone mode use Import. For volunteers and HAMs (amateur radio operators): Copy as text and paste it into a stake-wide text or email, or send the JSON and have them open it in the portal's Comms sheet page (forms/comms.html), which keeps it on the phone offline.

    


    

      
Phones on the EOC Wi-Fi — new reports and Send to EOC

      
Anyone on the EOC Wi-Fi scans the QR code on the wall (or opens the address below) and gets this EOC's phone page: New Disaster Report / Supply Order / Work Crew Log with a Send to EOC button (no files, no app needed), and the steps to send reports saved in the Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM app's Outbox out in the field. Reports arrive here within seconds, marked Phone / browser. When more than one EOC runs on the same network (a drill), each phone picks which EOC to send to. Phones are clients only; this computer is the server.

      

        
        
        
      

    


    

      
Wall Display — TV, projector or spare monitor

      
A read-only big board for everyone in the room: event and PACE level in force, reports received, which units have and have not reported, homes / injuries / volunteers totals, open supply orders, next SITREP due, the latest arrivals and the comms status. It refreshes itself every 10 seconds from this server. Nothing on it can be clicked, so it is safe to leave up all shift.

      

        
        
        
      

      
    
        
  1. Plug the TV in as a second screen on any PC (an HDMI, High-Definition Multimedia Interface, cable), or use any laptop / Chromecast / smart-TV browser on the EOC Wi-Fi.
    2. 
        
  2. Open the wall URL above in any browser on that screen, then press F (or F11) for full screen.
    3. 
        
  3. Set the PACE level in force and the event details above whenever they change — the wall shows them within 10 seconds.
    4. 
        
  4. Keep the roster complete: the wall's unit board is only as good as the roster (Roster tab, with expected report windows).
    5. 
        
  5. Options on the URL: ?refresh=5 seconds, ?sitrep=4 hours between SITREPs (overrides the event's interval on this screen only), ?latest=16 rows of arrivals.
    6. 
      

      
Needs the networked server (standalone mode has no wall). If the server stops, the wall says so and keeps the last picture.

1. PACE levels: when and how to change

        
PACE stands for Primary, Alternate, Contingency and Emergency. These are four independent ways to move reports to and from the EOC (Emergency Operations Center). When one fails, everyone steps down to the next. Step back up only after the higher path is proven to work again.

Who changes the level

        
    
          
  • The stake Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist (emergency communications) recommends
  • →
    • 
          
  • The Stake President decides (the bishop, for a single-ward net)
  • →
    • 
          
  • The ERD (Emergency Response Director) or the specialist announces it on the net
  • →
    • 
          
  • The EOC operator sets it on this page
    • 
        

        
Operators never change the level on their own. If you see a trigger below, tell the specialist straight away: what failed, and when.


        
When to step down one level

        
Step down when any one of these is true:

        
    
          
  1. Two attempts on the current path fail within 15 minutes. A text or email is not delivered, the Wi-Fi has no internet, a Winlink session will not connect, no gateway answers, or no one answers the net.
    2. 
          
  2. One form takes longer than 30 minutes to reach the EOC because the path is congested.
    3. 
          
  3. The Stake President declares it on the specialist's recommendation. Examples: a known cell tower or fiber cut, a county EOC announcement, or the county tasking our ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service) operators.
    4. 
        

        
At the EOC it usually shows up like this:

        
    
          
  • Units on the Roster turn OVERDUE with nothing arriving.
    • 
          
  • Team Leaders or volunteers say texts and emails will not send.
    • 
          
  • The drop email stops bringing in files.
    • 
          
  • Winlink Express cannot connect.
    • 
          
  • No one answers the net.
    • 
        


        
How to step down: EOC checklist

        
    
          
  1. Log it and report it. Add a Note saying what failed, when, and whom you told. Tell the specialist.
    2. 
          
  2. Set the level when the Stake President decides. Go to Setup, Active event details, PACE level in force, then Save Event Details. 
    The header, the wall display and the SITREP header all change, and a Note records the time.
    3. 
          
  3. Confirm the announcement. Check that the ERD or specialist has announced the level on the net, and repeat it at every check-in.
    4. 
          
  4. Tell senders the new path. Put one line in the Comms Sheet's this event only box, for example: Level C since 14:05. Send forms by Winlink to the EOC address. Voice on the stake net. Save it, then export the sheet to the other EOCs.
    5. 
          
  5. Tell the EOC above you. The next SITREP header carries the level. If the change matters to them now, send a short message on the path that still works.
    6. 
          
  6. Change how you work. See the list below.
    7. 
        


        
What changes for the EOC operator at each level

        
    
          
  • P Save emailed or texted files to the EOC Drop Folder. Send the SITREP by email.
    • 
          
  • A The same, over whatever internet you have. Keep an eye on the green Drop Folder bar.
    • 
          
  • C Reports arrive by Winlink auto-ingest. Keep Winlink Express connecting to the radio gateway, and watch the blue bar. When the gateway is down, collect mail with a Vara FM RMS Post Office session and keep a Vara FM P2P (peer-to-peer) session open. For a voice report from the net:
            
      
              
    • Mark the unit with ☎ on the Roster.
      • 
              
    • Add a Note.
      • 
              
    • Type the report into a form, as described in Receiving reports.
      • 
            
    
            Send the SITREP and roll-up by Winlink radio to the parent EOC's Winlink address.
    • 
          
  • E Type paper forms in the same way (the forms at /forms/dr, /forms/so, /forms/wcl, then Send). Keep blank paper forms stocked: add ?blank=1 to a form address and tap Print. Print the SITREP. Put the roll-up or handoff file on a USB drive for the runner or the county liaison. If the EOC has to move, export a handoff before you leave. The Stake President decides whether to move to the backup EOC.
    • 
        


        
When to step back up

        
Only after a test message has gone all the way round on the higher path and the EOC has confirmed it. Until then, stay at the lower level, even if the higher path seems to be back. In the first hours after a disaster, congestion comes and goes.


        
How to step up: EOC checklist

        
    
          
  1. Ask for a test on the higher path. For example, a volunteer emails a form file to the drop email (P or A), or a HAM sends a Winlink message over Telnet (A).
    2. 
          
  2. Confirm the round trip. When the test arrives, reply on the same path and make sure your reply arrives. Add a Note, for example: Round-trip test on A OK at 15:20.
    3. 
          
  3. Report it. Tell the specialist. The specialist recommends and the Stake President decides.
    4. 
          
  4. Set and announce. Set the new level and save. Update or clear the Comms Sheet's event line. Make sure it is announced on the net.
    5. 
          
  5. Keep collecting from the lower path. Files saved on phones and queued Winlink messages will still arrive as paths come back. They are filed under the same event, and nothing is counted twice.
    6. 
        


        
Good to know

        
    
          
  • Volunteers never need to know the level. Their instruction is always the same: fill the form, save it, and send it if you can. Otherwise, give it to a HAM or read it to your Team Leader.
    • 
          
  • The two hops can be at different levels. For example, the field can be at C while the EOC still reaches the Area by internet. Winlink stores messages until they are picked up. This page holds one level: the one the Stake President announced for the stake. Write the Area link's level in a Note and in the SITREP.
    • 
          
  • The EOC Wi-Fi works at every level. It needs no internet, so phones in the EOC send straight to the EOC whatever the level, and field workers' Outboxes empty the moment they reach it.
    • 
          
  • We use the county's level names, so the stake liaison at the county EOC can report our status without translating.
    • 
          
  • The paths and numbers for each level are on the Comms tab: points of contact, Winlink addresses, gateways in order, and nets.
    • 
        

      


      

        
2. Start of an activation

        
    
          
  1. Start the server. Double-click Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM EOC Server on the desktop. Give the address it prints (like http://10.1.2.3:8080) to the other operators on the same Wi-Fi. If Windows asks about the firewall, allow private networks.
    Without the server, the page runs standalone: one operator, no auto-ingest and no wall display.
    2. 
          
  2. Setup, This EOC: enter the level (Satellite, Stake or Area), the EOC name, the units it covers, and the parent EOC (name, email and Winlink address). Add the EOC location (phone forms use it) and, if you want a join-Wi-Fi QR code on the wall, the EOC Wi-Fi name and password. Click Save EOC Identity.
    3. 
          
  3. Setup, Open a New Event: enter the name, type, area and start date.
    4. 
          
  4. Setup, Active event details: enter the Stake President or incident lead, your name, the operational period, the PACE level in force and the SITREP interval leadership sets (2, 4, 8, 12 or 24 hours) with who directed it. Click Save Event Details.
    5. 
          
  5. Roster: add every ward (or every stake, at Area level) with its leader, phone and expected report window.
    6. 
          
  6. Comms tab: check that the sheet is current. Add any event-only lines on Setup.
    7. 
          
  7. Start Winlink Express and connect at the current level. Check the blue Winlink bar and the green Drop Folder bar.
    8. 
          
  8. Wall display on a TV (section 10). Its QR code is how phones in the room send reports.
    9. 
        

      


      

        
3. Reading the screen

        
    
          
  • Header: event name, PACE level, clock and time left in the operational period. The PACE button shows the level in force and opens section 1. Help opens this guide. You can also press ? on the keyboard.
    • 
          
  • Missionary bar: yellow until a Disaster Report answers the missionary question. Green when every report that answered says yes. Red when any report says no: tell the Stake President at once.
    • 
          
  • Server bar: green (connected) means every operator on the server shares the data. Amber (connection lost) means the server stopped answering: check its window. No bar means standalone: this browser only, no Winlink auto-ingest, no wall display.
    • 
          
  • Blue bar: Winlink auto-ingest. Green bar: internet Drop Folder; it also counts files whose reports were already on file ("· 2 already on file"). Click either bar to check or change its folder.
    • 
        

        
What each tab is for:

        
    
          
  • Overview: alerts, totals, the unit tracker, and roll-ups received from other EOCs.
    • 
          
  • Work Items: everything the reports ask somebody here to do or decide, with an owner, a status, the next action and the decision. See section 5. An item that came up in a roll-up from a lower EOC shows from <that EOC>: open it and press Reply to <EOC> when you have the decision; the reply downloads as a .json file to send back the way their roll-up came, and their Message Center closes the item with your decision. A reply that reaches this EOC (Drop Folder, Winlink attachment, dragged onto this page, or the Import button on Setup) closes the items this EOC set UP.
    • 
          
  • Roster: units, their leaders, expected report windows and status.
    • 
          
  • Disaster Reports: reports, and the SITREP generator.
    • 
          
  • Supply Orders: consolidated items and the phone script.
    • 
          
  • Work Crew Logs: crews and hours.
    • 
          
  • Messages: every arrival, its status, and which EOC it came through.
    • 
          
  • Notes: the action log.
    • 
          
  • Comms: the EMCOMM Comms Sheet.
    • 
          
  • Setup: this EOC, events, event details and PACE level, roll-up, shift handoff, Comms Sheet editor and wall display.
    • 
        

      


      

        
4. Receiving reports

        
    
          
  • Winlink: with the server running, Church forms received by the chosen callsign appear within 10 seconds. Choose it once: click the blue watcher bar, type the callsign (it lists the ones on this computer) and choose the Winlink Express folder in the folder browser; it is saved for every start. The EOC reads Winlink's messages where they are (C:\RMS Express\<callsign>\Messages\; RMS Express, for Radio Message Server, is Winlink Express's old name), only those that arrived after the event began, and never moves them. In standalone mode, drag the message's .mime file from that folder onto this page.
    • 
          
  • Phones on the EOC Wi-Fi: anyone in the EOC, with or without the app, scans the QR code on the wall (or opens the phone page address on Setup). They tap New Disaster Report / Supply Order / Work Crew Log, fill it in and tap Send to EOC. It is filed within seconds, marked Phone / browser. No files and no app needed. The form fills in the EOC location, because phones cannot share their GPS (Global Positioning System) location over the EOC Wi-Fi.
    • 
          
  • Reports saved in the Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM app: a field worker who filled in forms with no signal joins the EOC Wi-Fi, opens the app, taps Outbox, then Send to EOC, and scans the wall QR code (or types the address under it). Their phone shows a green "received" screen; their GPS from the field comes with the report.
    • 
          
  • More than one EOC on the same network (a drill): the phone lists every EOC with its name and event, and the report goes to the one picked.
    • 
          
  • Text, email, AirDrop or USB: save the volunteer's .txt file into the EOC Drop Folder (desktop shortcut), or drag it onto this page. It is filed within 10 seconds; Drop Folder files move to Ingested. A report that is already on file is not added twice: a note says so ("Already on file at the EOC"), and nothing else changes. Sending the same report again is safe.
    • 
          
  • Roll-ups and Comms Sheets (.json files) from other EOCs come in the same ways: Drop Folder, Winlink attachment, dragged onto this page, or the Import buttons on Setup. They merge automatically.
    • 
          
  • Voice or paper reports: open the phone page (Setup, Open the phone page) on this computer or any phone on the EOC Wi-Fi, tap New for the form, type the report in and tap Send to EOC. Then mark the unit with ☎ on the Roster. In standalone mode (no server) this page cannot take a typed-in report: add a Note, mark ☎, and have a HAM send the form by Winlink.
    • 
          
  • Duplicates are skipped automatically. The same report arriving twice (by Winlink and in a roll-up, for example) appears once.
    • 
        

      


      

        
5. Working the reports

        
    
          
  • Status: click a row's status badge to move it from NEW to ACK to ASSIGNED to DONE. Every screen updates.
    • 
          
  • 📄 Open shows the whole form as the volunteer filled it, with Print, Copy text and Save .txt.
    • 
          
  • Missionaries not accounted for: the red bar and Overview alert need immediate action. Tell the Stake President.
    • 
          
  • OVERDUE on the Roster means a unit has passed its expected window with nothing received. Call the bishop or Team Leader. If they report by voice, mark ☎.
    • 
          
  • Supply Orders: items from every unit are added together. Generate Phone Script reads them out for a call.
    • 
          
  • Work Items: the worklist. Every report filed adds the items it calls for on its own: missionaries not accounted for, injuries or deaths, each assistance needed answer, every Supply Order awaiting approval, a crew without its safety briefing, and a roster unit that goes overdue. Add your own with the box at the top (a phone call to return, a shelter to confirm, a decision the Stake President must make). Click an item to set who owns it, its status (OPEN, IN WORK, UP, DONE), the next action, a due time and the decision when it is made; each change is logged with the operator's name. The filter buttons are the worklists: Life safety, Missionaries, Supplies, Facilities, Assistance, Overdue units, No owner. UP means the item waits on the line above you: it goes with the next roll-up and lands open on that EOC's list, naming yours. No Council / Area EOC open? UP items go up only as SITREP Section I. Phone or email the welfare line for anything urgent and write their answer on the item. The SITREP carries every open item and the decisions recorded in the period (section I); the wall shows the open count and the high-priority items.
    • 
          
  • Notes: log every decision, every PACE change and every message you send up. Use Export as TXT to save the log.
    • 
          
  • Copy as TSV pastes any table into a spreadsheet.
    • 
        

      


      

        
6. SITREP and the lines above you

        
    
          
  1. Generate the SITREP: go to Disaster Reports and click Generate SITREP. The header carries the event, this EOC and the PACE level in force.
    2. 
          
  2. Send it on the current level's path. Click Copy, then:
            
      
              
    • At P or A, email it. From a Satellite EOC: to the Stake EOC. From a Stake EOC: to both lines above you, the council line (the Area Seventy's coordinating council) and the welfare line (the local Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager (WSRM), then the Area Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager (AWSRM)), plus the Council / Area EOC if one is open. Setup holds the addresses.
      • 
              
    • At C, send it as a Winlink message to the same addresses that have one (the welfare line's; the council usually has email only, and gets it when any internet returns).
      • 
              
    • At E, print it and send it by runner, county liaison or satellite phone.
      • 
            
    
          
    3. 
          
  3. Send on schedule. The Church asks for the first assessment as soon as possible, then "regular status reports" to the Area Seventy. EOC leadership sets how often on Setup (SITREP every 2, 4, 8, 12 or 24 hours, and who directed it), on the schedule the Area Seventy or the AWSRM asks for. Until they set one, the screens show "SITREP interval not set: ask EOC leadership". The Disaster Reports tab and the wall show when the next one is due, counted from the last SITREP generated.
    4. 
          
  4. Send a roll-up with each SITREP. Go to Setup and click Export Roll-up JSON, keeping "only new" ticked. Attach the file to an email or a Winlink message over Telnet (P or A) or a Winlink message by radio (C), or carry it on USB (E), to the EOC above you: the Stake EOC from a Satellite, the Council / Area EOC from a stake when one is open. A roll-up is for an EOC that merges it; the two lines get the SITREP text.
    No Council / Area EOC open? UP items go up only as SITREP Section I. Phone or email the welfare line for anything urgent and write their answer on the item.
    5. 
          
  5. Forward every Disaster Report and Supply Order to the welfare line as it is logged. In Winlink Express:
    1. Open the received form message.
    2. Click Forward.
    3. To: the welfare line's Winlink address from Setup (Welfare line Winlink address: the WSRM, then the AWSRM).
    4. Click Send.
    Do this for every Disaster Report and Supply Order as it is logged. Supply Order approvals come back down the welfare line.
    6. 
          
  6. Check on lower EOCs. Roll-ups from them show on Overview and in SITREP section H, so you can see who has gone quiet.

7. Emergency Events

        
    
          
  • Every report, roster entry and note belongs to the active event. All operators and all new arrivals use it.
    • 
          
  • For a new emergency, use Setup, then Open a New Event. The old event keeps everything and can be switched back to, exported or closed from its row.
    • 
          
  • Rename any event at any time. Close an event when it is over; it stays on file. You can delete only an empty event.
    • 
          
  • Closing an event clears the Comms Sheet's this event only lines. The standing sheet stays.
    • 
        

      


      

        
8. Shift change and backups

        
    
          
  • Shift change, networked: the incoming operator opens the same server address. Nothing needs to move. Walk them through open items, the PACE level and the next SITREP.
    • 
          
  • Shift change, standalone or moving the EOC: go to Setup and click Export Handoff JSON. The incoming operator clicks Import Handoff JSON, which replaces the active event's data.
    • 
          
  • Backups: the server writes one every 15 minutes to the backups folder. Click Backup Now on Setup for an extra one, or insert a USB drive for an immediate copy.
    • 
          
  • Never use Clear to start a new emergency. Open a new event instead.
    • 
        

      


      

        
9. EMCOMM Comms Sheet

        
    
          
  • It lists points of contact, each EOC's Winlink address and drop email, Winlink gateways in the order to try them, and voice nets.
    • 
          
  • Edit it on Setup and view it on the Comms tab. Print it for go-bags, or use Copy as text to paste into an email or Winlink message.
    • 
          
  • Export sheet JSON and send it to the other EOCs. Their servers take it on board automatically, and the newer sheet wins.
    • 
          
  • Volunteers and HAMs can open the same file on the portal's Comms sheet page.
    • 
          
  • Use this event only for temporary changes, such as a net moved to simplex or the current PACE instructions.
    • 
        

      


      

        
10. Wall display

        
    
          
  • The wall display needs the server. Open it with the 📺 link in the header, or from Setup, Wall Display. Put it on a TV or projector and press F for full screen.
    • 
          
  • It shows the event, the PACE level, units reported and waiting, totals, open supply orders, latest arrivals, comms and notes. It refreshes every 10 seconds, and nothing on it can be clicked.
    • 
          
  • A phone with no app can add an EMCOMM EOC home-screen icon from the phone page. It works only on this Wi-Fi.
    • 
          
  • Its main QR code opens this EOC's phone page: new reports with Send to EOC, and the address the Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM app scans to send its Outbox. If the EOC Wi-Fi name and password are on Setup, a second QR code joins the phone to the Wi-Fi (the password is shown on the wall; leave it blank on Setup to keep it off).
    • 
        

      


      

        
11. When something is wrong

        

          
What you seeWhat to do

          
            
Phones cannot open the phone page, or Send to EOC failsThe phone must be on the EOC Wi-Fi, not cellular. Allow the Windows firewall prompt for Python on private networks. Guest Wi-Fi often keeps devices apart; use the EOC's own router. Reports stay in the phone's Outbox until they go through.

            
Amber connection-lost bar, no green bar, or other operators cannot connectStart Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM EOC Server and use the address it prints. Make sure everyone is on the same Wi-Fi, and allow the Windows firewall prompt.

            
Winlink messages are not appearingClick the blue bar. Check the callsign chosen there: it must be the station the messages are sent to. Check the folder: C:\RMS Express. Check that Winlink Express actually connected. As a fallback, drag the .mime file onto the page.

            
A Drop Folder file is not filedIt must be the .txt saved by a Worker form, or a .json roll-up or Comms Sheet. Click the green bar to check the folder. Look in Ingested, because it may already be filed.

            
"Duplicate skipped"This is normal. That report is already on file.

            
Reports are filed under the wrong eventGo to Setup, Emergency Event, and switch to the right event. New arrivals follow the active event.

            
The wall display says the server is offlineThe server window was closed. Start it again. The wall keeps the last picture until then.

            
Paths keep failingFollow section 1: log it, tell the specialist, and step down when the Stake President decides.

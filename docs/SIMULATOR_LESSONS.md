# Disaster emergency communications (EMCOMM) lessons 2024 to 2026: what broke, what worked, what the simulator rehearses

Research behind the simulator's drill injects. Compiled 2026-10-30 by Claude (deep research over six parallel source sweeps: hurricanes, wildfires, earthquakes, floods, backup communications technology, faith-based response), reviewed into the first developed scenario the same day (a community's own, kept beside its export; the public example, Copper Creek Dam, carries the same lessons). The section "Prioritized simulator enrichments" at the end maps each lesson to the inject that rehearses it; every scenario inject that carries a `lesson` field cites this document. Acronyms used throughout: EMCOMM (emergency communications), EOC (Emergency Operations Center), SITREP (situation report), PACE (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency), WSRM and AWSRM (Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager; Area Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager), ERD (Emergency Response Director, this project's local-plan title for the handbook's welfare and self-reliance specialist), HAM (amateur radio operator), H&W (health and welfare), AUXCOMM (auxiliary communications, the civil-authority volunteer radio program), VARA (the sound-card modem Winlink uses on FM, frequency modulation, and HF, high frequency, radio), VHF (very high frequency), LEO (low-earth-orbit satellite), WEA and IPAWS (Wireless Emergency Alerts; Integrated Public Alert and Warning System), PSAP (public safety answering point, a 911 center), NG911 (Next Generation 911), NC (North Carolina), NCEM (North Carolina Emergency Management), VIPER (Voice Interoperability Plan for Emergency Responders, North Carolina's statewide trunked radio system), DANA (Spanish *depresión aislada en niveles altos*, an isolated upper-level low, the storm type behind the Valencia floods), EOP (Emergency Operations Plan), FBI (Federal Bureau of Investigation), PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder), SMS (Short Message Service, text messages), ETA (estimated time of arrival), CSV (comma-separated values), PDF (Portable Document Format).

**Independent volunteer project, not an official Church product.** Sources are public after-action reviews, government and press reports; figures marked "snippet only" or "gap" could not be read in full. No private individual's contact details appear here.

---


Across the disasters of 2024 to 2026, emergency communications (EMCOMM) failed in the same three ways regardless of hazard or country: the cellular network died from cut fibre backhaul and exhausted backup power rather than from fallen towers and then stayed dead for days to weeks in terrain-constrained areas; public alerting failed on the human side (an unsent or late alert, an untested system, an absent decision-holder) far more often than on the radio side; and the official intake of needs, resources and missing persons collapsed under roughly tenfold volume, producing duplicate well-checks, false "fulfilled" statuses and missing-persons counts that fell by an order of magnitude once verified. Hurricane Helene (27 Sep 2024) is the anchor case: **4,562 cell sites down at the peak**, 90 percent of sites still dark in two North Carolina counties a week later, 19 emergency call centres rerouted, the state's public-safety radio saturated for hours at a time, and satellite terminals that reached county Emergency Operations Centers (EOCs) only "several days into the communication blackout" ([Wireless Estimator](https://wirelessestimator.com/articles/2024/unprecedented-outage-no-hurricane-has-knocked-out-more-cell-sites-than-category-4-helene/); [NC SERT After-Action Review](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). The Noto earthquake (Japan, 1 Jan 2024), the Valencia floods (29 Oct 2024), the Eaton fire (7 Jan 2025) and the Kerr County flood (4 Jul 2025) each repeat one of those failure modes with different weather. What worked was pre-built and drilled: Taiwan's Hualien response opened a shelter with tents and hot meals two hours after the quake because the county and a faith-based organization had rehearsed it; Hawaii cleared its tsunami zones in four hours; mountaintop repeaters with power that outlasted three days carried the only traffic moving in western North Carolina. For a stake-level EOC of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the lessons that do not depend on government budgets are concrete: assume no cellular, no 911 and no internet for 72 hours and drill that way; keep one address-keyed list of who is accounted for; give every request a visible status and merge duplicates; verify before relaying; publish a health-and-welfare traffic policy before the net opens; pair every satellite terminal with a named operator and a power plan; hold volunteers and goods until there is a receiving point with an inventory. The last section turns these into fifteen prioritized simulator enrichments with injects for a four-hour drill.

## Cellular failed from fibre and fuel, not towers, and stayed down for weeks

The outage pattern of 2024 to 2026 was almost never structural. In Helene the Federal Communications Commission's (FCC) Disaster Information Reporting System (DIRS) counted **4,562 cell sites out across five states on 28 Sep 2024**, against the previous record of 1,051 for Ida ([Wireless Estimator](https://wirelessestimator.com/articles/2024/unprecedented-outage-no-hurricane-has-knocked-out-more-cell-sites-than-category-4-helene/)). On 1 Oct the FCC's county table showed Yancey County at 20 of 22 sites out (90.9 percent) and Mitchell at 18 of 20 (90 percent), with North Carolina's causes split 303 transport, 338 power and only 3 physical damage ([FCC Communications Status Report, 1 Oct 2024](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-406055A1.pdf)). A September 2026 preprint pooling the daily DIRS reports found damage accounted for 1.1 percent of outages, power 63 percent and transport 36 percent, with North Carolina's transport share rising from 7 percent on 27 Sep to **85 percent by 19 Oct** as generators were refuelled but the fibre stayed cut ([arXiv 2609.10944](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10944)). Chatham County's emergency management director told Congress that at day seven Yancey and Mitchell still had 77 and 90 percent of sites down, that the failure crossed "all cellular carriers, including FirstNet," and that the sites had no alternative transport such as satellite or microwave ([Newton testimony, 9 Sep 2025](https://democrats-energycommerce.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-energycommerce.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/09.09.2025_testimony_newton.pdf)). More than 1,700 miles of fibre were destroyed and most sites were not restored until about 19 Oct, three weeks after landfall ([Asheville Watchdog via Biltmore Beacon](https://www.biltmorebeacon.com/news/helene-created-an-unprecedented-communications-blackout-a-year-later-vulnerabilities-remain/article_ec1a5f5e-0117-4c24-a32e-3c77486df24a.html)). The FCC's own January 2025 letter gives the planning number: typical cell-site batteries last **four to eight hours**, fixed generators 12 to 48 hours, and only Verizon has generators at more than 90 percent of macro sites while others have them at "a small percentage" ([FCC DOC-409030A2](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-409030A2.pdf)).

The same mechanism recurs everywhere the terrain constrains routes. On the Noto Peninsula, power and transmission-line breaks accounted for about **97 percent of base-station outages**, coverage in the six worst municipalities fell to 20 to 30 percent of normal, 839 stations were out on 3 Jan, and about 90 percent of Japanese base stations have less than 24 hours of battery ([arXiv 2608.11925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11925); [Cabinet Office 2024 White Paper, Special Feature 2](https://www.bousai.go.jp/en/documentation/white_paper/pdf/2024/02_SF2.pdf)). In Türkiye in 2023, two-thirds of base stations were rooftop-mounted, only 35 percent had fibre backhaul, generators ran three to four hours, and connectivity "dropped to nearly zero within 24 hours because power problems worsened"; replacement cell trucks took more than 36 hours to arrive because they were not classed as first responders ([arXiv 2410.13977](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13977v1)). Vanuatu lost its national network when one fibre landing station in Port Vila was damaged ([ETC Vanuatu](https://www.etcluster.org/emergency/vanuatu-earthquake-december-2024), snippet only); Hualien lost 172 base stations to power cuts in a single valley with 36 still out a week later ([Taipei Times, 11 Apr 2024](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2024/04/11/2003816266)); Colombia's Chocó quake in August 2026 cut fibre for about four and a half days ([Colombia One](https://colombiaone.com/2026/08/10/cell-phone-internet-colombia-earthquake/)). Jamaica's Hurricane Melissa recovery was slower than Helene's: half of Digicel's 925 sites were still dark ten days after landfall, 503 of them with no commercial or alternative power, and theft of fuel and equipment stalled restoration ([Digicel update #1](https://www.psoj.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Digicel-Jamaica-Update-HURRICANE-MELISSA-1.pdf); [ETC Sitrep #2](https://new.etcluster.org/sites/default/files/documents/ETC%20Caribbean-Hurricane%20Melissa%20Sitrep%20%2302%2020251103.pdf)). Even where the network physically survived it collapsed under load: Istanbul's three carriers "buckled" for up to 15 minutes after the April 2025 M6.2 from a call surge alone ([Turkish Minute](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/04/23/mobile-networks-collapse-after-istanbul-earthquake-echoing-failures-seen-in-2023-disaster/)), and Türkiye 2023 saw voice traffic rise 9,150 percent in the first half hour ([arXiv 2410.13977](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13977v1)).

Emergency call-taking failed with the network or under the call volume. In Helene, **19 western North Carolina 911 centres went offline** and were rerouted to 23 others; statewide volume rose 55 percent over three days with a 586 percent single-day spike; Madison County's centre "could not receive 911 calls for nearly a month" and was answered from Chatham County more than 225 miles away until a satellite cell-on-light-truck restored it, "the first time this solution was used for 911 call delivery anywhere in the State" ([NC 911 Board AAR, Aug 2025](https://it.nc.gov/documents/nc-911-board-hurricane-helene-after-action-report/open)). Yancey County's centre "went silent" at 10:51 on 27 Sep with landlines and internet already down and radio calls taking "eight to 10 tries" ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/hurricane-helene-evacuation-warnings-yancey-county-north-carolina)). Valencia's 112 service saturated from about 14:00 on 29 Oct 2024, registered 19,821 calls that day and left **10,216 unanswered**, with only 20 percent answered in the first hours ([Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/comunitat-valenciana1/la-generalitat-confirma-el-colapso-del-112-en-la-dana-no-pudo-atender-la-mitad-de-las-llamadas); [Ara](https://en.ara.cat/valencia/valencia-s-112-hotline-left-8-290-calls-unanswered-the-day-of-the-dana_25_5451500.html)). Even the professional radio layer saturated: North Carolina's statewide trunked system (VIPER) "reached the point of total saturation and at times became largely unusable," and "several county emergency managers reported experiencing delays of over an hour when attempting to share or request information" ([NC 911 Board AAR](https://it.nc.gov/documents/nc-911-board-hurricane-helene-after-action-report/open); [NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). In Kerr County, Texas, out-of-area responders' radios took "several days to be reprogrammed to talk to each other" and no local official held overall command for the first 24 hours ([Firehouse](https://www.firehouse.com/rescue/water-ice/news/55307067/emergency-officials-were-asleep-as-floods-raged-in-tx-hill-county)). Buncombe County's EOC "used pen and paper, and responders relied on word of mouth and in-person shelter check-ins" ([Asheville Watchdog via Biltmore Beacon](https://www.biltmorebeacon.com/news/helene-created-an-unprecedented-communications-blackout-a-year-later-vulnerabilities-remain/article_ec1a5f5e-0117-4c24-a32e-3c77486df24a.html)). The state's own recommendation is the operating rule for any local EOC: train emergency managers to run "all their functions without any communications for up to 72 hours" ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)).

## Alerts failed on the human side: unsent, untested, or decided too late

Public warning failed in both directions, and almost never because the radio path was broken. In Valencia the national weather service had a red warning out at 07:31 on 29 Oct 2024 and the river authority flagged the Poyo ravine at 12:20, but the regional cell-broadcast alert (ES-Alert) was sent at **20:11**, after a compiled timeline puts 156 dead and 37 critical, roughly 84 percent of the final toll; a first draft at 17:45 that told people to stay indoors had been rejected as too aggressive, the sent text only advised against travel, and a technician testified the system had gone 18 months without a test ([DANA timeline, secondary compilation](https://benjajaja.github.io/dana-timeline/); [Spanje Vandaag](https://spanjevandaag.com/29/10/2026/dana-ramp-twee-jaar-later-schuldvraag/)). Two years later the same region sent two preventive ES-Alerts the afternoon before DANA Adam's heaviest rain, telling people to go to "elevated places or upper floors" ([Demócrata, 17 Oct 2026](https://www.democrata.es/en/comunitat-valenciana/why-the-alarm-has-sounded-today-on-the-mobiles-of-valencia-and-alicante-what-the-es-alert-of-the-adam-flood-says/)). In Kerr County the National Weather Service (NWS) pushed a Wireless Emergency Alert (WEA) at 01:14 on 4 Jul 2025 and a flash flood emergency at about 04:03, but federal records show the county sent **no Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS) message that day**; its opt-in CodeRED was requested at 04:22, told it needed a supervisor's approval, and went out between 05:01 and 05:34, while the county judge, sheriff and emergency management coordinator were out of town, asleep or sick until about 04:20 to 05:30 ([NBC 5 Investigates](https://www.nbcdfw.com/investigations/fema-records-kerr-county-didnt-send-eas-flood-warnings-phones/3882922/); [KSAT timeline](https://www.ksat.com/news/local/2025/08/03/flood-timeline-alerts-emergency-response-and-pivotal-moments-in-july-4-tragedy-in-the-hill-country/)). The federal inspector general's review found NWS staff tried to reach the coordinator and sheriff between about 03:38 and 04:42 and could not get through ([News4SA summary of OIG-26-017-I](https://news4sanantonio.com/news/investigations/inspector-general-nws-issued-warnings-hours-before-kerr-county-flood-adequately-staffed); the primary PDF is encrypted and was not read). Of 43 counties with Helene deaths, 29 sent no IPAWS message; Yancey, with 11 deaths and twice the per-capita rate of any other North Carolina county, issued no evacuation order while its emergency manager softened NWS wording ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/hurricane-helene-emergency-alerts-north-carolina); [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/hurricane-helene-evacuation-warnings-yancey-county-north-carolina)).

The Eaton fire is the cleanest documented decision gap. County data show ignition at 6:17 p.m. on 7 Jan 2025, a first evacuation order for zones east of Lake Avenue at 7:26 p.m., no new orders requested between 1:12 a.m. and about 3:00 a.m., and the first order west of Lake Avenue at **3:25 a.m.**, about 25 minutes after Incident Command gave direction; all or all but one of the 19 deaths were west of Lake Avenue, median victim age 77 ([Eaton Fire Synopsis of Findings and Timeline](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1192777_AAR_EatonFireSynopsisofFindingsandTimeline.pdf); [Timeline Overview](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1191567_EatonFireTimelineOverview.pdf)). The McChrystal Group review found "no single point of failure" but "outdated, unclear and contradictory" policies, one Office of Emergency Management staffer sending alerts for three fires, aircraft grounded 27 minutes after ignition, and a trending social-media phrase, "the fire is moving east," that "fostered some complacency" on the west side ([Board presentation, 30 Sep 2025](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/bos/supdocs/208035.pdf); [Highlights and Recommendations](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1192778_AAR_HighlightsandRecommendations.pdf)). Two days later the county's alerting tool sent a Kenneth fire evacuation order to "nearly 10 million" phones up to 40 miles away because the staffer's narrow polygon was not uploaded to IPAWS, "possibly because of local network outages," and the software did not warn that the polygon was missing; echo alerts followed for days from overloaded carrier systems ([Spokesman-Review/LA Times, 12 May 2025](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/may/12/investigation-into-false-evacuation-alerts-sent-du/)). Utility Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS) also removed the power that WEA towers, sirens and care-facility notification depend on: the review lists PSPS-darkened towers and smoke attenuation as reasons residents received no alert, and on Maui only one outdoor siren within the Lahaina burn perimeter was operable ([Highlights and Recommendations](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1192778_AAR_HighlightsandRecommendations.pdf); [FSRI Lahaina Phase 3](https://d1gi3fvbl0xj2a.cloudfront.net/2025-01/Lahaina_Fire_Forward_Looking_Report_010924_Final.pdf)). Korea's March 2025 fires show the same lag in a rural, elderly population: a nursing-home vehicle was engulfed less than ten minutes after entering the highway following an evacuation notice issued about two and a half hours after fire entered the county, while an inland village that ordered evacuation two to three hours earlier lost no one ([Korea Daily](https://en.koreadaily.com/worst-wildfires-in-korean-history-claim-27-lives-as-firefighting-struggle-to-contain-blaze/)). Opt-in channels failed by design: only 11.2 percent of Rio Grande do Sul's population was registered for the state's SMS alerts, and loudspeakers in Canoas announced evacuation "hours after the surrounding neighborhoods had already been submerged" ([Agência Pública](https://apublica.org/2024/05/sistema-de-alertas-sobre-tragedia-no-rio-grande-do-sul-falhou-dizem-especialistas/); [COP30 site](https://cop30.br/en/news-about-cop30/floods-in-rio-grande-do-sul-exposed-the-climate-crisis)). Poland's audit office found the national alerts sent 11 to 15 Sep 2024 were identical across 11 voivodeships and that a voivode never passed on the Stronie Śląskie dam breach ([NIK summary](https://www.podatki.biz/artykuly/nik-zarzadzanie-kryzysowe-w-czasie-powodzi-do-poprawy_16_63491.htm)).

Where warnings reached people early and someone acted, the death toll stayed low even when losses were large. Hawaii's transport department received the tsunami warning at 14:43 on 29 Jul 2025, released state workers 13 minutes later, opened an extra pass, cancelled contraflows, and confirmed all evacuation-zone roads clear by 19:00 against a 19:17 first-wave estimate; its lessons were about public confusion over watch, advisory and warning and about keeping people outside the zone off the roads ([HDOT briefing, 28 Aug 2025](https://hidot.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/House-Informational-Briefing-Aug-28-2025-2.pdf)). Taiwan's warnings went out within about 20 seconds of the Hualien mainshock, the county centre reached full activation in 17 minutes and a shelter opened at 90 minutes ([Frontiers in Built Environment, Enokida et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2025.1593942/full)), though the alert threshold left Taipei unwarned and was changed afterwards ([Wikipedia, 2024 Hualien earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hualien_earthquake)). Kentucky's February 2025 flood had a rare Day 2 "High Risk" outlook, 77 warnings and 19 swift-water teams pre-positioned, which produced "more than a thousand water rescues in the first 24 hours" ([NWS Louisville](https://www.weather.gov/lmk/KYFlooding); [Louisville Public Media](https://www.lpm.org/news/2025-02-16/a-thousand-water-rescues-in-24-hours-widespread-flooding-douses-kentucky)). Pakistan's Punjab authorities evacuated 2.5 to 3 million people ahead of the 2025 riverine floods, which the United Nations credited with avoiding 2022's scale ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/09/1165864)). Jasper's residents, drilled beforehand, cleared the town along a single highway with no collisions after a 10:00 p.m. order ([Jasper Municipal AAR](https://jasper.municipalwebsites.ca/UploadFiles/Docs/JasperWildfireComplexMunicipalAfterActionReview_FY68y8.pdf); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10637891/jasper-national-park-wildfire-evacuation-2024/)). The common thread in every review that examined alert text is the same fix: name the zone, give a timestamp, say what to do, and make sure a named person with authority and a named backup is awake and reachable.

## Satellite arrived days late and only worked with a trained operator; amateur radio carried welfare traffic but no one led the nets

Low-earth-orbit (LEO) satellite internet became the alternate path within days of Helene, but days is the operative word. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) reported 40 Starlink systems available on 30 Sep 2024, "one Starlink… per county Emergency Operations Center," then 60, with later counts of 67 kits to North Carolina and about 500 deployed by private groups ([FEMA advisory, 30 Sep 2024](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/USDHSFEMA/2024/09/30/file_attachments/3015191/FEMA%20Advisory%20%20Biden-Harris%20Administration%20Continues%20Whole-Of-Government%20Response%20to%20Hurricane%20Helene%2C%20Over%203%2C500%20Federal%20Personnel%20%28September%2030%2C%202024%29.pdf); [WFMY](https://wfmynews2.com/article/weather/hurricane/helene/fema-providing-starlink-satellite-systems-to-western-north-carolina/83-aeb7dcf0-dacd-4c7a-bedc-7d6610e67c0f)). The figures for total terminals (40, 60, 67, 140, 500, "10,000+") come from different scopes and vendor claims and cannot be reconciled from the sources found. The state review's verdict is double-edged: LEO service "proved particularly effective, but only when local emergency responders had access to them… they arrived several days into the communication blackout," and "it took days to place [alternate] communications resources (Starlinks and satellite phones) into the impacted counties… These delays kept NCEM in the dark about ground truth"; competition with neighbouring states for satellite equipment was "particularly acute" ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). A nonprofit bought five terminals at a hardware store for a Tennessee fire department before state-requested units arrived ([Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2024/10/30/critical-connections-satellites-role-in-the-wake-of-hurricane-helene-november-2024/)). The deeper limit showed in Japan: carriers put **660 Starlink units** into Noto evacuation centres and medical teams, but community-centre staff found them "not necessarily easy to use" in cold and snow, with outdoor installation needing clear sky, instability from slight shifts, abandoned attempts, and a shortage of people who knew how to operate them; the author's conclusion is that "the existence of communication equipment and residents' ability to use it are not necessarily equivalent" ([MIC 2024 White Paper](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/joho_tsusin/eng/whitepaper/2024/pdf/09_Section-2.pdf); [arXiv 2608.11925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11925)). Power draw is 40 to 75 watts continuous for a standard dish, and trees, buildings and terrain block it ([D4H blog](https://www.d4h.com/blog/improving-emergency-management-communications-with-starlink-satellite-internet); [ResponseMesh](https://www.responsemesh.com/post/starlink-for-first-responders-what-it-can-and-can-t-do-in-the-field)); in Taiwan's Tiansiang valley the Ministry of Digital Affairs had to combine LEO and medium-earth-orbit terminals to get lines of sight ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2024/04/11/2003816266)). Under a hostile government the terminal is a liability: in Myanmar, Starlink was unlicensed, soldiers reportedly seized kits, and a Sagaing resident paid for two hours on a clandestine unit to learn, more than 24 hours after the quake, that it was widespread ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/myanmar-earthquake-internet-shutdown/); [Reuters via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2025/04/09/in-myanmar-internet-shutdowns-hinder-earthquake-aid-response/)). By Hurricane Melissa, Jamaica was counting Starlink in the hundreds (700 donated units for the security forces, disaster office, fire brigade and health ministry) and a carrier had paired with Starlink direct-to-cell so customers could text when towers were down ([Jamaica Observer, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2025/11/11/telecoms-network-rebounding-jamaica-receives-700-starlink-units-melissa/); [Jamaica Gleaner](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20251029/flow-starlink-partner-boost-connectivity-after-hurricane-melissa)). Direct-to-cell in Helene, Milton and the Los Angeles fires was text-only and "best-effort" under FCC emergency authority ([PhoneArena](https://phonearena.com/news/FCC-allows-T-Mobile-and-SpaceX-to-launch-satellite-service-in-Helene-hit-areas_id163439); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/t-mobile-makes-satellite-texting-available-through-spacexs-starlink-for-la-amid-fires/)); its documented value is alert broadcast and SMS, not voice or data.

Amateur radio was, in the words of the American Radio Relay League (ARRL), "the only functional tool for communication until cellular on wheels trucks and satellite internet terminals were deployed after several days of near total grid-down" in western North Carolina, operating through North Carolina's credentialed auxiliary communications programme (NC AUXCOMM) at EOCs, mountaintop repeaters on backup power, high-frequency (HF) nets on 7232 and 3923 kHz, and "unusually high" Winlink traffic ([ARRL, 4 Oct 2024](https://www.arrl.org/news/ham-radio-serving-southeast-us-recovery-efforts)). The Mount Mitchell repeater "carried welfare traffic for hundreds of families, resource requests, and medical coordination" for several days; a Tennessee net request produced donated batteries that restored the Viking Mountain repeater covering much of the impact area ([DeKalb ARES](https://dekalbares.org/stories/helene/); [ARRL](https://www.arrl.org/news/ham-radio-serving-southeast-us-recovery-efforts)). A Swannanoa resident with a licence and home satellite internet asked the fire department for supplies over the repeater and the neighbourhood had chainsaw chains the next day ([DC Report](https://www.dcreport.org/?p=34290)). Winlink's operational pattern was the formal written channel into state EOCs: Florida's state station and its Shared Resources (SHARES) address auto-forwarded to the State Watch Office, Georgia's emergency management station and tactical net address, and North Carolina's "Winlink on SHARES channels to pass traffic to the State EOC" in the AUXCOMM credential ([ARRL Helene Storm Updates](https://www.arrl.org/news/helene-storm-updates); [NC ARES and AUXCOMM](https://www.ncarrl.org/ares/)).

Three failure modes sit beside those successes. First, channel time: Georgia's net told operators to "use short text to describe the event or damage rather than use any Winlink Template messages" ([Georgia ARES net activation](https://gaares.org/?p=1644), indexed text only), and on 9 Oct 2024 the Winlink Development Team asked "that all non-essential traffic on the Winlink system be suspended, including tests, weekly nets, etc., until after Hurricane Milton," because gateways were still carrying Helene traffic ([Halifax ARC repost](https://www.halifax-arc.org/2024/10/09/the-winlink-development-team-hurricane-milton/)). Second, health-and-welfare (H&W) demand against agency channels: the state AUXCOMM coordinator said North Carolina Emergency Management "will not accept inbound H&W traffic" through its emergency-support channels, the national traffic nets took it with slow turnaround, and the Raleigh club recorded that "getting radio traffic into and out of the area, especially H&W messages, proved very difficult" ([RARS Helene page](https://www.rars.org/helene.php)). Third, leadership of spontaneous nets: "no single organization led the response. Groups of hams acted on their own initiative," and ad hoc nets "organized themselves in the first 72 hours" ([Ham Weekly](https://daily.hamweekly.com/2024/10/ham-radio-and-the-helene-aftermath/); [DeKalb ARES](https://dekalbares.org/stories/helene/)). Hardware mattered more than mode: Black Mountain's backup VHF repeater died when "most of those towers exhausted their batteries" and the generator failed, and the fix was a 400-watt solar panel modelled to run "two or three days, which is what we would have needed to do during Helene" ([WUNC, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.wunc.org/2025-09-29/hurricane-helene-took-down-first-responders-primary-radio-network-now-theyre-reviving-an-old-technology)). Family Radio Service (FRS) handhelds "worked poorly across the mountainside's distances" and General Mobile Radio Service (GMRS) repeaters became the community fill; GMRS licences rose 130 percent in the year after Helene and new repeaters in Yancey and Buncombe were used again in the January 2026 winter storm ([DC Report](https://www.dcreport.org/?p=34290); [WFDD/BPR, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.wfdd.org/2026-09-30/how-grassroots-radio-networks-are-helping-wnc-prepare-for-future-communications-outages)). In the Texas floods ten hams were assigned to search teams and "dozens of health and welfare messages" moved because "circuit overload" blocked family contact, but no formal activation call came at first in Kerrville ([ARRL, 9 Jul 2025](https://www.arrl.org/news/amateur-radio-volunteers-serving-during-texas-floods); [Hill Country Community Journal](https://www.hccommunityjournal.com/article_4cdf4821-970f-4c64-a13c-fb98f0cbea9c.html)). Outside the United States the record is thinner: in Türkiye 2023 most emergency traffic was VHF coordination for search teams, and individual amateurs could help only if "embedded" with accredited teams ([ARRL, Feb 2023](https://www.arrl.org/news/view/amateur-radio-active-in-turkey-and-syria-following-earthquakes)); in Japan the national league's role in Noto was to publish and protect emergency frequencies, with no handled emergency traffic reported ([hamlife.jp, 1 Jan 2024](https://www.hamlife.jp/2024/01/01/exz-freq-20240101/)). No source documented Winlink traffic in any 2024 to 2026 earthquake or wildfire, no ARRL, AUXCOMM or Winlink after-action report with message counts was found for any event, and Meshtastic mesh use in Helene rests on single-official and secondary accounts only ([Tallahassee Informer](https://www.tallahassee-informer.com/2025/03/12/monticello-council-considers-new-emergency-communication-system-proposal/)); those are gaps, not evidence of absence.

## Requests rose tenfold and lists quadruplicated: intake failed before supply did

The commodities were there; the tracking was not. By 30 Sep 2024, day four, FEMA had shipped 1.9 million meals and 1 million litres of water, run a C-17 air bridge into Asheville and had more than 900 search-and-rescue personnel on the ground ([FEMA advisory, 30 Sep 2024](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/USDHSFEMA/2024/09/30/file_attachments/3015191/FEMA%20Advisory%20%20Biden-Harris%20Administration%20Continues%20Whole-Of-Government%20Response%20to%20Hurricane%20Helene%2C%20Over%203%2C500%20Federal%20Personnel%20%28September%2030%2C%202024%29.pdf)). Yet the state review found request volume "nearly ten times higher than in previous responses" in a WebEOC resource module released months earlier that lacked search, filtering and status notifications; the platform slowed for two days; "responders received false delivery or fulfillment alerts… once a request appeared to be fulfilled, those resources were often delayed, never arrived, or were diverted"; warehouse staff double-entered data while dispatching more than 100 trailers; **58 percent of surveyed responders said resources were not allocated effectively**; and duplicate requests sent resources "to areas that had already received support, while other communities experienced extended delays." One participant: "There was a log jam of resource requests in logistics… critical needs that sat or were lost in the shuffle, and there was no established way to track anything once it was ordered… We had to rely on backdoor relationships" ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). Buncombe County had no tracking system at all, stood up its own WebEOC about two weeks in, logged 5,759 requests to the state, and shifted intake among "radios, personal cell phones, and mandating email intake with no internet access" ([Buncombe County AAR](https://www.buncombenc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4231/After-Action-Report---Tropical-Storm-Helene-2024)). The review attributes part of the volume to "a lack of local awareness about available resources and protocols for requesting them," which means a published catalogue and request protocol reduces load before the event ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). Noto shows the same loop from the receiving end: Wajima's mayor had 2,000 meals for 10,000 evacuees, helicopter loads were hand-carried because they were not palletized, the digital distribution system "didn't start until January 5," and cardboard beds decided on day 10 arrived on day 16 ([Nippon.com, Quality of Life](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g02476/)).

Missing-persons counts followed the same curve in every country: a call log mistaken for a list, then an order-of-magnitude collapse once each name was verified. Buncombe reported about 600 unaccounted for on 1 Oct 2024 while a crowd-sourced list held almost 2,000 names ([NPR](https://text.npr.org/nx-s1-5134991)); the state found "the lack of a unified missing persons tracking system created critical delays in search prioritization… well checks conducted at the same homes three to four times," with search teams on two unintegrated platforms and 211 adapted mid-response as the missing-persons system ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)). Buncombe's Register of Deeds improvised a Family Assistance Center that ran welfare checks to 15,982 addresses with 2,685 volunteers by 25 Oct, while "multiple organizations reportedly compiled separate missing persons lists without collaboration" ([Buncombe County AAR](https://www.buncombenc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4231/After-Action-Report---Tropical-Storm-Helene-2024)). Lahaina's list went from more than 3,000 names to an FBI-validated 388 (24 Aug 2023), 22 (22 Sep) and 4 at 100 days ([Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2023/08/25/maui-releases-list-hundreds-people-unaccounted-after-lahaina-fire/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2023/11/18/us/maui-fires-100-days/index.html)). Valencia's "1,900 missing" was a crisis-meeting minute reading "2,500 missing in the 112" (the interior minister said these were calls from people who could not find relatives) and fell to **89 by 5 Nov** once police verified each report ([Maldita.es](https://maldita.es/malditateexplica/20241101/1900-desaparecidos-comunidad-valenciana/); [Maldita.es, 6 Nov](https://maldita.es/malditobulo/20241106/cifra-oficial-desaparecidos-dana/)). Kerr County went from 161 missing on 9 Jul 2025 to 3 on 19 Jul after "extensive follow-up work among state and local agencies"; neighbours had meanwhile compiled their own list on Facebook ([KVIA/CNN](https://kvia.com/news/2025/07/19/texas-officials-revise-number-of-people-missing-in-kerr-county-following-catastrophic-flooding-down-to-3/); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/07/20/nation/number-missing-floods-texas-county-drops-nearly-100-3)). The lesson for a status report is to carry two numbers, reported and verified, and to key every entry to an address.

Volunteers and donations arrived faster than any intake. Valencia's spontaneous volunteers choked the motorways for about 72 hours until a single sign-up channel, a staging hub at a science museum, and police-escorted buses moved about 10,000 people on 2 Nov and 14,000 by 6 Nov under the rule "que nadie vaya por sus propios medios" (nobody goes by their own means) ([El Español, 7 Nov 2024](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20241107/coordinan-voluntarios-ayudar-dana-valencia-nadie-vaya-propios-medios/899160239_0.html)). North Carolina "struggled to handle the influx of volunteers and donations… securing a warehouse to manage donations was difficult, and once one was identified, there was no inventory system" ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)); six months later a Tennessee church still held about 10,000 toothbrushes and more than 20 pallets of water while what people needed was money for drywall and shingles ([Christianity Today, 27 Mar 2025](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/03/donated-clothes-hurricane-helene-second-disaster/)). The Church's own Area Seventy said "unsolicited truckloads of goods overwhelmed one community. A donated warehouse was used to sort goods for about three months" ([Church News podcast, 5 Nov 2024](https://www.thechurchnews.com/podcast/2024/11/05/episode-213-hurricanes-milton-helene-florida-north-carolina-lds-response-disaster-relief-elder-quinn-millington/)). Ishikawa asked individuals not to send aid and general volunteers to stay away at first because the few open roads were congested ([Cabinet Office SF2](https://www.bousai.go.jp/en/documentation/white_paper/pdf/2024/02_SF2.pdf)). The spontaneous air volunteers of Helene succeeded on speed (Operation Helo reports about 4,000 missions, 450 evacuations and 2 million pounds delivered from its own self-made EOC, with tactical teams finding "local leaders (usually volunteer fire chiefs) to assess community needs") but built parallel command structures the Cajun Navy itself now asks to formalize ([Vertical Aviation](https://verticalavi.org/magazine-articles/operation-helo-changing-the-course-of-disaster-response/); [United Cajun Navy House submission](https://transportation.house.gov/UploadedFiles/House_TI_Submission_Cajun_Navy.pdf)). Misinformation consumed the remaining capacity: "disinformation and/or misinformation negatively impacted response efforts" for **43 percent of surveyed state responders**, "the spread of unverified information regarding specific incidents or needs resulted in the misallocation of limited response resources," and FEMA paused door-to-door work for staff safety ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open); [AP, 8 Oct 2024](https://abcnews.go.com/US/wireStory/fema-prepares-hurricane-milton-battles-rumors-surrounding-helene-114624609)). Utah's closest precedent is the 2020 Magna earthquake, when a rumour of a magnitude 9.0 "spread through credible sources across church faiths" within 30 minutes and had to be corrected ([Deseret News, 17 Mar 2021](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2021/3/17/22325628/earthquake-lessons-from-a-year-ago-no-one-is-coming-to-save-you-utah-salt-lake-county-magna-disaster/)).

## Shelters and the vulnerable became the second disaster

In the earthquakes the shelter killed more people than the shaking. Noto's direct deaths numbered 228; certified disaster-related deaths passed them at 261 by December 2024 and reached 397 by July 2025, with 147 of them in the first month; an assessment of 115 indirect deaths cited distress, severed lifelines and evacuation-centre conditions ([Nippon.com, One Year On](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02234/); [Nippon.com, Failures… Reforms](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01152/failures-of-noto-disaster-relief-drive-major-reforms-japan%E2%80%99s-revised-laws-target-infrast.html); [Wikipedia, 2024 Noto earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Noto_earthquake)). About 60 percent of the direct dead were in their seventies, eighties or nineties, and just over 10 percent died of hypothermia ([Cabinet Office SF2](https://www.bousai.go.jp/en/documentation/white_paper/pdf/2024/02_SF2.pdf)). Shelters were unpartitioned and without water, toilet kits "ran out quickly," evacuees walked out to portable toilets in snow and dark, and in Wajima's Monzen district rehabilitation teams found residents breaking bones on steps between toilet and bathroom and not reporting it; their triage of 27 centres in one day is the model for sizing beds, toilets and handrails per shelter ([Peace Winds America](https://peacewindsamerica.org/earthquake-relief-japans-noto-peninsula-situation-changed-three-months/); [Frontiers in Disaster and Emergency Medicine](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/disaster-and-emergency-medicine/articles/10.3389/femer.2024.1387704/full)). Japan's response was a December 2024 guideline aligned to Sphere standards (one toilet per 20 people, 3.5 square metres per person) and a May 2025 package of six amended laws extending relief to people at home or in cars, mandating victim registers and local stockpiles for isolated communities ([Nippon.com, Failures… Reforms](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01152/failures-of-noto-disaster-relief-drive-major-reforms-japan%E2%80%99s-revised-laws-target-infrast.html)). Heat was the mirror image: about two million Japanese were told to evacuate for the Kamchatka tsunami in 40 °C with 1,360 heat ambulance cases nationwide on 30 Jul 2025, and Kumamoto's July 2026 quake put about 15,000 people in 400 centres with a suspected heatstroke death and cars that could not run air-conditioning for lack of fuel ([Wikipedia, 2025 Kamchatka earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kamchatka_Peninsula_earthquake); [Wikipedia, 2026 Kumamoto earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kumamoto_earthquake)). Taiwan's counter-example is organizational, not technical: Tzu Chi and the township had drilled shelter operations before the quake, a government-civil society contact network was running on a messaging app within an hour, and tent partitions were up at two hours because groups "provided services without waiting for government instruction" ([Nippon.com, Quality of Life](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g02476/); [Frontiers, Enokida et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2025.1593942/full)).

In the United States the first 48 hours exposed untested plans and lost sites. Buncombe's Shelter Operations Plan was completed in September 2024 "but it was never tested" (the exercise was scheduled for October); two pre-positioned church shelters were lost to flooding within a day; "unexpectedly large shelter populations coupled with utility outages led to deficiencies in food and water, backup power, medical supplies, sanitation," and partner agreements "proved difficult to activate in a timely manner as the regional demand divided the partner organization's capacities" ([Buncombe County AAR](https://www.buncombenc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4231/After-Action-Report---Tropical-Storm-Helene-2024)). Practitioners a year on reported "critical shortages occurred in the first 48 hours," adult incontinence and hygiene supplies hardest to source, and shelters that have since adopted satellite internet and mesh radios ([JEMS, Sep 2025](https://www.jems.com/major-incidents/one-year-later-disaster-shelter-lessons-from-hurricane-helene/)). Henderson County, which had run a shelter exercise the week before Helene, "didn't look at the EOP because we had exercised these components so much," and McDowell County began door-knocking the day the state emergency was declared ([EdNC, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.ednc.org/edncs-hurricane-helene-playbook-lessons-learned-in-emergency-management/)). Among North Carolina's local health departments, prior exercises were the strongest predictor of a successful activation ([NCALHD AAR](https://ncalhd.org/Docs/Password%20Site/TS%20Helene%20AAR%20FINAL%20LHD.pdf)).

The people who died in the fires were disproportionately old and immobile, and the failures were roll-call and trigger failures. In Altadena, two homebound men (one an amputee, one with cerebral palsy) died after three 911 calls between 6:03 and 6:35 a.m. while the county's paratransit service was "ready to respond" from 4:00 a.m. but never called; the county had scrapped its disability registry about 2016 and dissolved its access-and-functional-needs committee in 2020 without finishing an evacuation plan ([LAist, 14 Jul 2025](https://laist.com/news/climate-environment/despite-three-911-calls-two-homebound-disabled-men-died-in-the-eaton-fire-waiting-for-rescue); [LA Times via Yahoo, 2 Apr 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/botched-fire-evacuations-l-county-161140646.html)). At one senior community most night staff had never trained in emergency procedures, trained managers went home as the winds rose, there was no recall list, and a roll call at the destination found two residents missing, one aged 100 found two hours later on a dark third floor with flames visible ([FireRescue1/LA Times, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.firerescue1.com/emergency-management/reports-highlight-alert-failures-seniors-left-behind-during-calif-wildfire)). What worked was deputies physically leading transit buses into the fire zone and a facility evacuated within the first hour ([Eaton Fire Synopsis](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1192777_AAR_EatonFireSynopsisofFindingsandTimeline.pdf)). A disability-policy consultant's alternative to a registry is "a dedicated non-911 line for people with mobility problems" and tested plans ([LA Times via Yahoo, 2 Apr 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/botched-fire-evacuations-l-county-161140646.html)); the Lahaina review's version is expanded Community Emergency Response Teams (CERT) with "specific evacuation plans for vulnerable populations, including the elderly, disabled, and those without transportation" ([FSRI Phase 3](https://d1gi3fvbl0xj2a.cloudfront.net/2025-01/Lahaina_Fire_Forward_Looking_Report_010924_Final.pdf)). The Church's 2011 planning guide already asks stakes to keep "lists of members with special needs" and "help to locate and reunite family members" ([Stake and Ward Emergency Planning Guide, 2011](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/bc/content/shared/content/english/pdf/welfare/stake-ward-emergency-planning-guide-eng.pdf?lang=eng)); the evidence says the list is useless unless a drill makes someone call the names.

## The Church's response ran on hotlines and priesthood lines, not radio

The published record of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in 2024 to 2026 shows a consistent sequence that a stake simulator should model faithfully. In Helene the North America Southeast Area's Disaster Response Committee, headed by an Area Seventy, worked through stake presidents: supply trucks staged before landfall; a "week zero" limited response by a few affected stakes; a "week one" surge drawing on bishops' storehouse resources; **11 command centres by 7 Oct 2024**; and a final tally of 21,284 volunteers, 311,256 hours and 10,348 homes over six weeks ([Church News podcast, 5 Nov 2024](https://www.thechurchnews.com/podcast/2024/11/05/episode-213-hurricanes-milton-helene-florida-north-carolina-lds-response-disaster-relief-elder-quinn-millington/); [Church Newsroom, 7 Nov 2024](https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/latter-day-saints-hurricanes-helene-milton)). Intake was a telephone hotline: **638 volunteers from 73 stakes fielded more than 17,000 calls and "created work orders so disaster response organizations could identify people in need,"** with the Crisis Cleanup hotline named as a supported partner ([Church Newsroom, 7 Nov 2024](https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/latter-day-saints-hurricanes-helene-milton)); that is the same shared work-order pool Team Rubicon and the Baptist conventions worked from ([Team Rubicon, 28 Sep 2024](https://teamrubiconusa.org/news-and-stories/hurricane-helene-how-to-get-help-assist-survivors/)). The Asheville stake president's first report, about day three, was that members were being reached by hand-digging roads, no passable road led into Asheville, some areas faced three weeks without power, there was no timeline for cell service, and there were "no reports of members injured, but some have been evacuated" ([Church News, 30 Sep 2024](https://www.thechurchnews.com/living-faith/2024/09/30/church-hurricane-helene-humanitarian-aid-relief-efforts/)). How that report reached the Area Seventy during the blackout is not described in any Church source found; the stake-to-area link under grid-down conditions is a documented gap, and no Church source describes radio, Winlink or satellite use by stakes in Helene.

Elsewhere the sequence is accountability first, then a deliberate hold. In Texas the Area Seventy described leaders who "first go through priesthood lines to learn the impact on members and the community," a flow from stake presidencies to ward leaders, Relief Society presidencies, "emergency response specialists, and JustServe specialists," and "a daily call at 11:15 a.m. with emergency authorities to detail needs for facilities and equipment," with members told "there will be opportunities to help, but we have to allow the authorities" ([Church News, 9 Jul 2025](https://www.thechurchnews.com/living-faith/2025/07/09/central-texas-kerrville-flooding-ministering-mercies-miracles)). Three weeks later "local members working in government and emergency response helped with real-time coordination and gave updates to local Church leaders" while cleanup stayed on hold at the authorities' request ([Church Newsroom, 29 Jul 2025](https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/service-highlights-central-texas-flooding-response)). In Los Angeles about 1,000 members were evacuated and more than 30 homes lost; a bishop sent a questionnaire asking whether members had water, food, clothing and shelter; 1,500 cases of water went out on day three; and the public was told to "volunteer through local organizations, until volunteers are needed directly" ([Deseret News, 10 Jan 2025](https://www.deseret.com/faith/2025/01/10/church-makes-buildings-available-for-shelter-provides-aid-as-california-fires-destroy-member-homes/); [Salt Lake Tribune, 13 Jan 2025](https://sltrib.com/religion/2025/01/13/la-fires-how-latter-day-saints-are)). Valencia is the one case of a stake "control center" at a meetinghouse acting as "a logistics base where other organizations can collect supplies," buying against "a list of essential items from local authorities," with vans arriving on day three and welfare missionaries "in constant contact" with the regional emergency centre ([Church Europe Newsroom, Nov 2024](https://news-europe.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/the-church-of-jesus-christ-of-latter-day-saints-supports-flood-relief-efforts-in-spain-valencia)).

Policy frames all of this. The General Handbook says the stake president "oversees Church emergency response and communications within his stake," may call welfare and self-reliance specialists "to oversee an emergency operations center" or manage communications, and reports "the status of members, missionaries, Church facilities, and the community" to the Area Presidency or assigned Area Seventy and the welfare and self-reliance manager, through a chain running ministering brothers and sisters, quorum and Relief Society leaders, bishop, stake president ([General Handbook ch. 22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/22-providing-for-temporal-needs?lang=eng)). The Emergency Communication Guidelines put the specialist in a recommending role, say Church headquarters runs amateur radio nets, tell wards and stakes not to buy or accept radio or satellite-phone equipment for meetinghouses, and estimate that about 80 percent of communications infrastructure typically returns within 72 hours ([Emergency Communication Guidelines](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/emergency-response/emergency-communication-guidelines?lang=eng)). The Procedures ask for an evaluation "as soon as possible" and then "regular status reports" with no interval, and describe operations centres that "record requests, map locations, assign teams, and track completion" ([Emergency Response Procedures](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/emergency-response/emergency-response-procedures-detailed-guidelines?lang=eng)). Two frictions follow. The 72-hour estimate was wrong by a factor of seven for the hardest Helene counties, so a stake PACE plan (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency) must plan for weeks at Contingency. And because state emergency management refused inbound health-and-welfare traffic on its auxiliary channels ([RARS Helene page](https://www.rars.org/helene.php)), the Church's own nets and Winlink are the realistic path for member-status reports; the Deland Florida Stake's description of amateur radio "between Wards, the Bishop's Storehouse and Church Headquarters when phone lines… are down" is the only stake-level example found and is undated. The term "Emergency Response Director (ERD)" used in this project is a local-plan title; no handbook document uses it, and project materials should give the handbook term first.

The large faith-based responders converge on mechanics a stake EOC can copy outright: a single intake, a two-to-three-person assessment team whose priority "is generally based on the people and circumstances and not necessarily the work," no work until "there is a signed work order release on file," a call to the homeowner the day before, certified sawyers with hard hats and one visibly marked leader per crew, and self-sufficient volunteer camps ([SBDR Recovery Assessment manual](https://ibsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/SBDR-Recovery-Assessment-2020.pdf); [SC Baptist Chainsaw manual](https://www.scbaptist.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Chainsaw-2021-SCBC-Version.pdf)). North Carolina Baptists on Mission had 7,000 volunteers registered within weeks and placed 13,000 from 48 states by Christmas, and reported that "damaged infrastructure initially made communication nearly impossible until mobile Starlink units were deployed… We couldn't communicate with volunteers or homeowners easily" ([Golden LEAF, 8 Oct 2025](https://goldenleaf.org/baptists-on-mission-help-families-return-to-their-homes-in-western-north-carolina-through-funding-partnerships-and-volunteers/)). A survey of 373 western North Carolina volunteers six to eight months after Helene found 93 percent were themselves exposed, about half depressed or anxious, and "communication failure is the strongest predictor of the PTSD indicator for poor sleep," with aid-distribution volunteers carrying the highest burden ([Natural Hazards Center brief, 2026](https://hazards.colorado.edu/uploads/quick_report/HEW10_Ryan_Research%20Brief.pdf)). The volunteers the simulator trains are also survivors; the drill should make that visible.

## Conclusion

The 2024 to 2026 record changes what a stake EOC drill should test. The scarce resource in the first 72 hours was never a radio mode or a satellite dish; it was a decision made by a named, awake person, a list that one organization owned, and a status that flowed back to whoever asked. Every official review that praised a technology (Starlink "a home run," ham radio "the only functional tool," the NG911 reroute that lost no call with a dial tone) also recorded that the technology sat unused for days because nobody had placed it, trained on it or decided who would speak on it. A simulator that only degrades channels teaches operators to re-route messages; one that also withholds decisions, duplicates lists, feeds false fulfilments and rumours, and makes volunteers and trucks arrive unasked teaches the EOC to run the organization the reviews say was missing.

The second shift is temporal. Church guidance's 72-hour horizon and a four-hour drill both end long before the real failures bit: Yancey's cellular at day 21, Noto's isolated hamlets at day 19, Noto's 147 shelter deaths in month one, missing lists that took two to three weeks to reach "one list." A four-hour run cannot play that, but it can end with the question every review asks after the fact: who relieves this shift, where is the fuel, which terminal has an operator tomorrow, and what does the next SITREP say about verified versus reported. The enrichments below are built so that the scoreboard rewards those answers.

## Prioritized simulator enrichments

Priority reflects how often the failure recurred across events, how directly a stake EOC can act on it, and how cheaply the existing engine (`kind`, `need`, `after`, `reply`, `done.checks`) can express it. Times are drill minutes (T+) in a 240-minute run; the examples assume a sudden-onset scenario like the developed ones; place names are generic. Acronyms: EOC (Emergency Operations Center), ERD (Emergency Response Director, the project's local-plan title for the handbook's welfare and self-reliance specialist), WSRM and AWSRM (Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager, Area Welfare and Self-Reliance Manager), SITREP (situation report), PACE (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency), H&W (health and welfare), HAM (amateur radio operator), HF and VHF (high and very high frequency).

### 1. The blackout outlasts the drill: no scheduled step-up, and the relay dies

- **Evidence.** Helene: 4,562 sites down on 28 Sep 2024; Yancey and Mitchell still 77 to 90 percent dark at day seven; most sites restored only by about 19 Oct ([FCC, 1 Oct 2024](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-406055A1.pdf); [Newton testimony](https://democrats-energycommerce.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-energycommerce.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/09.09.2025_testimony_newton.pdf)). Black Mountain's backup repeater died when tower batteries were exhausted and the generator failed ([WUNC, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.wunc.org/2025-09-29/hurricane-helene-took-down-first-responders-primary-radio-network-now-theyre-reviving-an-old-technology)). Noto: 97 percent of base-station outages from power and backhaul; batteries under 24 hours ([arXiv 2608.11925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11925)). State recommendation: operate "without any communications for up to 72 hours" ([NC SERT AAR](https://www.ncdps.gov/division/emergency-management/ts-helene-after-action-review/open)).
- **Drill behaviour to provoke.** The EOC stops expecting the schedule to rescue it: it plans simplex and HF relays, keeps paper, rotates operators, and writes a 72-hour posture into SITREP #2 instead of assuming Alternate at T+165.

A drill without a disaster: the Simulator

The Simulator plays the wards, the mission office, the county and the line above against a real EOC (Emergency Operations Center). Reports arrive the way the PACE (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency) level sends them; every one starts a clock. At the end the room sees what went well and what to practice. Experimental: tell us what you find.

1Run a training session

  1. Start the EOC Server, then double-click Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM Simulator and open http://<laptop>:8090. It opens on the Session tab: the trainer's path.
  2. Before the meeting: load the pack, check the EOC, plan the scenario; rehearse a few minutes at 10×.
  3. Reset for the session: it opens a fresh drill event, and every EOC screen shows waiting for the trainer's GO.
  4. Brief the room, press GO (or Schedule one start time on every laptop). Speed 1× to 60×.
  5. Stop the drill (click twice), then the Hot wash tab, the after-action report and the debrief.

2Try it today: an invented community

The Juniper Plains Area is made up: 2 coordinating councils, 8 stakes, 51 wards and branches, invented leaders and drawn ward maps. No directory export is needed.

Copper Creek Dam failure, a Sunday in March at 07:50: an evacuation order below a seeping dam that fails at T+38. Four hours, 148 injects, 14 roles: a care center surrounded by water, sister missionaries not accounted for, Contingency when the flood takes the power, a rumour about a second dam.

3How it scores

  • Every report, card and work item is a need with a deadline: owned, closed, decision recorded.
  • A need met brings its resolution; one overdue brings the caller back.
  • Mark met credits what the room did by voice or on paper.
  • The scoreboard is on the Run tab and in every EOC's purple DRILL bar.

4Your own stake, safely

  • Your directory export and maps stay on the laptop, never online.
  • Names are pseudonymized by default; real contacts only with the Stake President's consent, recorded on the Setup tab.
  • Several EOCs? One simulator each; Schedule starts them together.

PACE Levels: Which Path, When to Step Down

PACE Situation Winlink Connection
P Cell + internet up Volunteers text/email files to the EOC. You: Open Session → Telnet
A Cell down, any internet anywhere Any connection that still reaches the internet. Files arrive by email or USB drive. Open Session → Telnet
C No internet; a gateway answers Data, 1. RMS Gateway: Open Session → Vara FM Winlink → the VHF/UHF (very/ultra high frequency) gateway on the Comms Sheet. Send as Winlink Message. No internet there? It may be held at the gateway for the CMS (Common Message Servers).
Voice: stake VHF/UHF net (repeater, then simplex); volunteers on FRS/GMRS (Family Radio Service / General Mobile Radio Service) to Team Leaders.
C “No CMS available” or no gateway 2. Vara FM RMS Post Office: Send as Post Office. 3. Vara FM P2P (peer-to-peer): Send as Peer-to-Peer; the recipient must connect. 4. Winlink Message over VARA HF (high frequency), 40m or 80m: only what must go. A wrong Send as stays in the Outbox. iPhone files: a USB-C/Lightning drive, or Send to EOC.
E Nothing connects, no one answers the net Runner: the printed form, or a blank form filled by hand, to the EOC; the operator types it in. Hold saved files; send when any path returns.
Addresses, gateways, Post Office, P2P frequency, nets: from the stake Comms Sheet — printed copy in the go-bag, or load the sheet file on your phone at …/forms/comms.html.
PACE rule: step down a level after two failed attempts in 15 minutes, or when the Stake President decides it. Step back up only after a test message round-trips. The stake Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist (emergency communications) recommends the level, the Stake President decides, and the Emergency Response Director (ERD) or specialist announces it. The county may call ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service) operators to county duty at its Emergency level; agree beforehand who stays with the stake.

THE PACE PLAN — FOUR INDEPENDENT PATHS, USED IN ORDER

PPrimary — cellVolunteers text or email the form file
AAlternate — any internetThe same over any Wi-Fi that still has internet: home, shelter, business, meetinghouse, Starlink
CContingency — radioHAM operators send forms by Winlink radio (VARA FM first, then VARA HF: the radio modems Winlink uses); voice on FRS/GMRS (Family Radio Service / General Mobile Radio Service) and the stake net

Matches the county ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service) plan where the stake sits.

Who decides: the stake Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist (emergency communications) recommends the level, the Stake President decides (the bishop for a single-ward net), and the Emergency Response Director (ERD) or the specialist announces it on the net.

Part of the stake cut off? A Satellite EOC in a ward building covers those wards and rolls up to the Stake EOC.

The Three Forms & What the Bishop Does

THE THREE FORMS

Disaster Report
Documents a damaged home or injured member. Routes to the Stake EOC, then up the welfare line (WSRM, AWSRM).
Supply Order
Requests food, equipment, or supplies. Needs ERD and welfare line approval (WSRM, then AWSRM) before it is filled.
Work Crew Log
Records volunteer crew hours and tasks completed. Gives the Stake President accountability data for reporting.

WHAT THE BISHOP NEEDS TO DO

  • Ensure at least one ward member is trained as Emergency Response Director
  • Identify your EMCOMM HAM operator (may be a ward or stake calling)
  • Put the forms’ web address (URL) and QR (Quick Response) code in ward emergency preparedness materials
  • Give the stake specialist your ward’s FRS/GMRS channel, HAM operator and Satellite EOC building for the stake PACE sheet
  • See that every volunteer keeps blank paper forms and a pen in the go-bag (portal → Paper forms)
  • Take part in the yearly 2-hour practice drill (card 9 explains a drill)
You do not need to understand the technology. The system is designed so that the Bishop gives direction and volunteers do the rest. The Stake EOC gives you accurate totals for your reports to the Stake President.
The Stake President decides: the PACE level (on the specialist’s recommendation); the SITREP (situation report) interval; opening a Satellite EOC; moving to the backup EOC; approving the stake PACE sheet; consent before a drill uses real contacts.

It covers the wards or the part of the community the Stake President assigns to it. One stake can have several.

The SITREP text to the Stake Center EOC (and anything the Stake President asked for by voice on the net).

Supply Orders are not approved here: they ride up in the roll-up and the welfare line answers through the Stake Center.

WORK ITEMS

Own what the satellite can do (a shelter check, a crew, a headcount). Anything for the Stake President or the welfare line: set it Up; it rides in the roll-up and arrives open at the Stake Center.

WHAT A DRILL IS

  • A drill is practice. The events are invented.
  • Every simulated report says SIMULATED.
  • Anything you send: tick THIS IS AN EXERCISE at the top of the form.
Real emergencies always come first. If a real one happens, say “real world”. The drill stops.

WHAT HAPPENS IN THE ROOM

1
The controller gives a short briefing. Listen for your role and your EOC (Emergency Operations Center).EOC operators: press I am ready on the purple drill bar.
2
The controller says GO. The drill clock starts.
3
Slips of paper arrive during the drill. Do what each one says (table below).
4
The controller says when the drill ends. Stop, then join the debrief (side 2).
Net cardFor a HAM (amateur radio) operator: read it aloud on the radio net.
Prompt cardA decision or a job for the role named on it.
Paper cardA report on paper. Carry it to the EOC; an operator types it in.

Other reports reach the EOC screens by themselves: from phones, by Winlink radio email, or as files.

What to bring: a phone, tablet or laptop, charged (no app? scan the code on the EOC wall); a pen; the card for your role (side 2).
Not sure what to do? Ask the controller. Write down what you did and the time. Do your best: mistakes are how a drill teaches.

FIVE WORDS TO KNOW

EOC — Emergency Operations Center: the room with a laptop, a radio and Wi-Fi that collects every report.
PACE — the order of ways to send: P Primary, cell phone · A Alternate, any internet anywhere · C Contingency, radio (Winlink by VARA FM, then VARA HF: the radio modems Winlink uses) · E Emergency, a runner with paper.

HAM / Winlink — a HAM is a licensed amateur radio operator. Winlink is email by radio: it carries the forms when phones and internet are down.

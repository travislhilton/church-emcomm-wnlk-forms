Changing Level

Down when two attempts fail in 15 min, a form takes over 30 min, or the Stake President declares it

Up only after a test message round-trips and the EOC confirms it — never because it “seems” back

The specialist recommends, the Stake President decides, the ERD or the specialist announces the level on the net; the EOC puts it in the SITREP header and logs every change

Three ladders, because forms, voice, and the hop up to the lines above are different jobs. The third column is the one people forget: the Stake EOC still has to get the SITREP and the Supply Orders to the AWSRM. Winlink makes this easier than it looks, because a message sent by radio sits in the central servers until the AWSRM picks it up by whatever connection they have, so the field hop and the Area hop can be at different levels at the same time. Forms are the record: Disaster Reports, Supply Orders, Work Crew Logs. Voice is coordination: an injury, a crew moving, a question for the EOC. Both ladders share the same four levels and the same network at each level, so one level is declared and every role knows what to do on both. Who decides it follows the Church's Emergency Communication Guidelines: the stake Welfare and Self-Reliance Specialist for emergency communications recommends the backup mode, the Stake President decides, and the ERD or the specialist announces it on the net. The state diagram on the right is the discipline: step down only on failure, step back up only after a confirmed round-trip test. The county plan uses the same vocabulary, so a stake liaison at the county EOC can say "we are at Contingency" and be understood.

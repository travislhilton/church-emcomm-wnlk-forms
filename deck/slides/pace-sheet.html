County coordination

The county ARES PACE plan runs one ladder per function. Its Email ladder is Facility ISP (internet service provider) → Starlink → Amateur Radio (Winlink) → Runner — the same boundaries as ours.

  • county EOC Winlink address — a concrete first target for forwarding a SITREP
  • County amateur repeaters — for the liaison, not for the stake net
  • Stake HAMs who are ARES members — the county tasks them at its Emergency level; plan the stake net around who remains
  • Ward FRS/GMRS channels checked against county GMRS use
  • County P25 (Project 25 digital radio), talk-around and NIFOG channels are public-safety resources — not part of this plan

Same level names as the county, so “we are at Contingency” needs no translation at the county EOC.

Blank sheet and full plan: docs/PACE_PLAN.md §6 — a path that has never been tested is not a path; drill every row yearly

Nothing on this sheet can be invented by the software. It is the stake's own numbers: addresses, frequencies, tones, channel assignments, who has the Starlink, which meetinghouse is the backup EOC. Once the specialist types the 6a numbers into the EOC Message Center's Comms Sheet, though, nobody retypes them: every operator sees them on the Comms tab, the wall shows them, the printed go-bag copy comes from the same sheet, and one small file carries them to every other EOC and to volunteers' phones. The county coordination column exists because the county already runs a PACE plan and already has a claim on ARES operators at its Emergency level. The stake plan has to know that in advance, not discover it on the day. Fill it in, print it, exercise every row once a year.

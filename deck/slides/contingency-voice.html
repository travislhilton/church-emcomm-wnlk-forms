Contingency — No Internet Anywhere

Radio Carries Both the Forms and the Voice

VARA FM carries the form

A full Disaster Report is under 2 KB — seconds on VARA FM, still fast on HF. The record arrives exactly as the volunteer typed it.

Voice carries the urgent

Injuries, hazards, “we need help now” — and the report from a Team Leader with no HAM in reach. FRS needs no license; a GMRS repeater does.

Stay off county frequencies

The county reaches amateur radio at its own Emergency level and tasks ARES hams. Keep the stake net off county, NIFOG (National Interoperability Field Operations Guide) and ARES channels; know which of our HAMs the county will call.

Voice sits at Contingency, not Emergency: a working radio path beside VARA FM. Emergency means no radio works at all

Contingency is the level this whole system was built for, so it gets its own picture. Two lanes run at once. The data lane is the Winlink path: file to the HAM by AirDrop or USB, template loaded, sent by VHF/UHF VARA FM to the local RMS gateway, then, if that gateway is down, the local VARA FM RMS Post Office, VARA FM P2P, and VARA HF to a gateway anywhere in the country. The voice lane is how coordination and reports move when there is no HAM within reach of a crew: volunteers on the ward FRS/GMRS channel to their Team Leader, Team Leaders to the stake amateur net, repeater first and the published simplex frequency when the repeater is down, and net control at the EOC types the fields in. The right-hand box is the point: both lanes end in the same Message Center and the same SITREP.

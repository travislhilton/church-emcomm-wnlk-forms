Communications Strategy

The PACE Plan

Four independent networks, stepped through as each fails — levels aligned with the county ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service) PACE plan

P

Primary

Cellular

Forms: volunteer fills the Worker Form, taps Share / Send, texts or emails the file to the Team Leader, ERD, or EOC Drop Folder.

Voice: cell call or text.

Network: cell carriers

Moved by: the volunteer

Cell works

A

Alternate

Any internet

Forms: volunteer joins any Wi-Fi with internet (home, shelter, business, meetinghouse, Starlink) and emails the same file. HAM: Load Data → Telnet over any reachable internet.

Voice: Wi-Fi calling, FaceTime, WhatsApp.

Network: any surviving internet, anywhere

Moved by: volunteer, Team Leader, HAM

Cell down; any internet anywhere

C

Contingency

Radio

Forms: HAM sends the Winlink template by VHF/UHF VARA FM to the local gateway, or by VARA HF to any gateway in the country.

Voice: volunteers on FRS/GMRS to the Team Leader; Team Leaders and HAMs on the stake VHF/UHF amateur net (repeater, then simplex). The operator types voice reports in.

Network: amateur VHF/UHF & HF, FRS/GMRS

Moved by: everyone with a radio

No internet: VARA FM, then VARA HF

E

Emergency

Runner

Forms: paper form, printed from the phone (Print) or a blank copy from the go-bag filled by hand, carried by runner to the Team Leader or EOC; the operator types it in.

Voice: runner, face to face.

Network: feet

Moved by: everyone

Nothing electronic works

Step down when

Two attempts fail in 15 min · a form takes over 30 min to reach the EOC · the Stake President decides it

Step back up when

A test message has gone round-trip on the higher path and the EOC confirmed it — not before

Who decides

The stake Welfare & Self-Reliance Specialist (emergency communications) recommends, the Stake President decides, the ERD or the specialist announces it on the net; the EOC logs it in the SITREP

Same four levels from the Stake EOC to the lines above (next slide). Volunteers never change what they do; Team Leaders and HAMs carry the switch

PACE is the framework the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), state emergency management and the military use: Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency. Two rules: move down only when the current path fails, and never use the same path twice. Our level boundaries match the county ARES plan: cellular is Primary, any surviving internet (Wi-Fi, Starlink) is Alternate, radio is Contingency, a runner is Emergency. Contingency covers both data and voice: Winlink VARA FM and HF carry the forms, while FRS/GMRS at ward level and the stake VHF/UHF amateur net carry urgent traffic and reports from Team Leaders who have no HAM in reach. Note the county's own Emergency level for public-safety voice is amateur radio — when the county gets there it tasks ARES operators, so the stake sheet records which of our HAMs are ARES members.

Cell Down, Internet Still Works

Why Cell Fails First

  • Cell towers carry only 2–4 hours of battery backup (no federal mandate since the Federal Communications Commission, FCC, withdrew the 8-hr rule after Katrina)
  • Network is immediately congested — 10–50× normal traffic at disaster onset
  • Physical towers are exposed to wind, flood, and fire
  • During Hurricane Sandy: 25% of cell sites in a 10-state area failed

What Changes Without Cell

The app still opens with no signal

Installed on the Home Screen in advance, it works offline — install it before the event

File transfer: any Wi-Fi with internet — email, or USB (SMS no longer reliable)

Volunteer joins any Wi-Fi that still has internet (home, shelter, business, meetinghouse, Starlink) and emails or messages the file; Wi-Fi calling for voice; or USB; or AirDrop / Android Nearby Share (Wi-Fi Direct, no cell needed)

Winlink: Telnet still works over wired internet

Fiber and cable backbone is significantly more resilient than cell; internet service provider (ISP) nodes carry 24+ hours of backup power

Minimum Equipment

  • Volunteers: Phone (form cached offline); USB drive
  • HAM station: PC + Winlink + wired internet

AirDrop and Android Nearby Share use Wi-Fi Direct — they work phone-to-phone with no cell or internet needed

Cell is almost always the first infrastructure to fail. Wired internet (fiber, cable) is considerably more resilient — backbone fiber has no powered nodes between endpoints, and ISP central offices run on extended generator backup. Plan file transfer without SMS from the start of any disaster operation.

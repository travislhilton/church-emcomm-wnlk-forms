Church of Jesus Christ EMCOMM Simulator — Drill the Whole Chain

A disaster scenario played against the running EOC server, with the stake’s real wards on the map and traffic that arrives the way real traffic arrives

What it plays

  • Reports from every playing ward, by the channel the PACE level in force selects: phone upload at P / A, a Winlink .mime into the Received folder at C, a paper card at E — every one marked THIS IS AN EXERCISE
  • Notes to the EOC log (aftershocks, bridges, the hospital, the county)
  • Net cards for the HAM operators to read on the stake net
  • Prompts to role players: the specialist recommends, the Stake President decides
  • Work items that land on the EOC’s list: the mission office, the county, the welfare line, the Stake President’s question

Clock at 1× to 60×; pause, jump, hold or skip any inject. PACE changes follow the scenario’s triggers and are pushed to the EOC header.

How a drill runs

  1. Setup: organization pack from the Church directory export; the roles the directory lacks (ERD, specialist, HAM) typed in and the must-have roles flagged until filled; privacy mode; what this simulator plays (a Stake EOC, a Satellite EOC’s wards, or the councils of a Council / Area EOC); the EOC’s address, the scenario, the speed
  2. Map: wards, buildings and severity bands; rings appear as reports reach the EOC
  3. Run: the clock (or a start time shared by every simulator), every EOC on the network and who pressed I am ready, the level in force, every inject with its minute; cards to hand out; what the EOC filed
  4. Roles: one printable card per role player, highlighted as its minute comes
  5. Scoreboard and after-action report: every report, card and work item creates a need with a deadline; met brings the resolution, overdue brings the caller back; the welfare line answers what the EOC sets UP; the report lists what to do better, by role
  6. Debrief for the room: Session tab step 5 opens projector slides: what went well, the score, one thing per role, the next drill’s date

Privacy: pseudonymized by default (invented first names, numbers and emails, same person = same pseudonym); real contacts only by an explicit choice with the Stake President’s consent. The pack and the map dataset stay on the laptop, never in the repository.

Scenarios

Copper Creek Dam failure is fully developed, in an invented community (the Juniper Plains Area: eight stakes, drawn ward maps, invented leaders): 07:50 on a Sunday in March, an evacuation order below a seeping dam that fails at T+38; 4 hours, 148 injects (47 of them consequences and replies), 14 roles, 30 hot-wash checks, 8 done-when checks. Meetings to cancel, a care center surrounded by water, missionaries not accounted for, Contingency when the flood takes the substation, Supply Orders up the welfare line and back down; and the failures of the real disasters of 2024–2026 built in: a dam rumour, a care facility with no transport, duplicate orders, walk-up volunteers.

Eight more hazards are outlined for the invented valley. A stake’s own scenario names its real places, so it lives beside its directory export, never in the repository, and goes to the drill’s participants in a private package with its own reference cards.

Tested by 180 automated checks against scratch Stake, Satellite and Area EOCs; the role card for the drill controller is Quick Reference Card 5. Seven pre-filled practice forms from this scenario ship with the Winlink templates.

The simulator is how a stake rehearses without a disaster: the EOC operators see real-looking traffic from their own wards, the HAM operators read the net cards, the Stake President gets the decisions the scenario puts in front of him, and the hot wash reads the Work Items list to show what was owned, when, and what was decided. Nothing from the organization data ever enters the repository.

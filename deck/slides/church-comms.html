The Response Structure

Bishop & Ward

The Bishop activates the ward response and deploys Helping Hands volunteers — identifiable by yellow vests. First boots on the ground, usually within hours of a disaster.

Stake President & ERD (Emergency Response Director)

The Stake President coordinates across multiple wards. The Emergency Response Director runs day-to-day operations from the Stake EOC, routing reports and supply requests upward.

AWSRM & Area Leadership

The Area Welfare & Self-Reliance Manager coordinates supply logistics across the entire area — multiple stakes, bishop's storehouses, and Church welfare resources.

What HAM (Amateur Radio) EMCOMM Adds

When cell towers and internet fail — often in the first hours — licensed HAM operators become the only reliable communication link between field teams and the Stake EOC.

📱 Volunteer fills the form in the app; sends it while cell or Wi-Fi works
📋 No service? Team Leader collects, hands off to a HAM, or reads it over FRS/GMRS (Family Radio Service / General Mobile Radio Service)
📻 HAM sends via Winlink radio; voice on the stake net
🖥️ Stake EOC receives & routes up to the AWSRM the same way

The same three Church forms, now with radio delivery built in.

This is the "why" slide. The Church already has a proven response structure — Bishops, Stake Presidents, ERDs, and the AWSRM. EMCOMM doesn't replace that structure; it gives it a communication backbone that survives infrastructure failure. The 2024 hurricane response numbers put the scale in perspective: tracking thousands of work orders when cell and internet are gone is exactly the problem this system solves.

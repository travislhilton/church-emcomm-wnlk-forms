Orientation

How a Report Travels: Field to the Lines Above the Stake

Five hands, one form: field → Satellite EOC → Stake EOC → the council line (Area Seventy) and the welfare line (WSRM, AWSRM). Only the network changes

P

Primary

Cellular

Volunteer texts or emails the file straight to the Team Leader, ERD, or EOC Drop Folder. In the EOC within 10 seconds. Stake → lines above: SITREP (situation report) by email, forms by Winlink Telnet.

A

Alternate

Any surviving internet

Cell is gone. Volunteer joins any Wi-Fi that still has internet (home, shelter, business, meetinghouse, Starlink) and sends the Outbox. A HAM who receives it sends by Winlink Telnet over that internet. Stake → lines above: same, over any reachable internet.

C

Contingency

Radio

Forms: file to the HAM by AirDrop or USB (Universal Serial Bus) drive → Winlink by VHF/UHF (very/ultra high frequency) with the VARA FM radio modem, or HF (high frequency). Voice: FRS/GMRS to the Team Leader → stake VHF/UHF net → EOC types it in. Stake → lines above: Winlink by radio into the CMS (Winlink's Common Message Servers); the welfare line picks it up by any connection.

E

Emergency

Feet

No radio works. Paper form (printed from the phone, or a blank one filled by hand) carried by a runner to the Team Leader or EOC; the operator types it in. Stake → lines above: liaison at the county EOC (county Starlink / sat phone) or courier.

What Never Changes

  • The three Church forms
  • The chain of command
  • The Winlink templates
  • The EOC Message Center and its records
  • What the volunteer does: fill, save, send

The specialist recommends, the Stake President decides, the ERD or the specialist announces the level in force on the net. Team Leaders and HAMs carry the switch. Volunteers never have to think about it.

P · A · C · E = Primary · Alternate · Contingency · Emergency — four independent networks, used in order, never the same one twice

This is the orientation picture for the whole deck. Top row: the five hands a report passes through, from a Helping Hands volunteer to the two lines above the stake: the council line (the Area Seventy) and the welfare line (the WSRM, then the AWSRM). A Satellite EOC, when one is open, sits between the field and the Stake EOC. There are two hops: field to Stake EOC, and Stake EOC to the lines above. The PACE plan covers both, with the same four levels. The four lanes are the four networks, in the order we use them: cellular, any surviving internet, radio, and feet. The grey note in each lane is the stake-to-the-lines-above version of that level; the key fact there is that Winlink's central servers hold a message sent by radio until the welfare line picks it up by whatever connection they have, so the two hops can be at different levels at once.

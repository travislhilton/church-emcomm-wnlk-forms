Connectivity Planning

What Fails, in Order, and the PACE Level for Each

Infrastructure fails in a predictable sequence: cell first, then wired internet, then satellite. Each step has its PACE (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency) level

P

Full
Connectivity

PACE: P · PRIMARY

Cell + wired internet + satellite all working normally.

Winlink: Telnet

Share: Email, SMS (text message), AirDrop

A

Cell Down,
Internet Works

PACE: A · ALTERNATE

Cell fails first — 2–4 hr battery backup, congestion, tower damage.

Winlink: Telnet (wired)

Share: Email, USB, local Wi-Fi

A

Internet Down,
Starlink Works

PACE: A · ALTERNATE

Wired internet out. Starlink’s orbital signal is unaffected — needs only 100W power.

Winlink: Telnet over Starlink

Share: USB, Wi-Fi Direct

C

All Internet Down,
VHF/UHF Radio

PACE: C · CONTINGENCY

No internet path. Amateur radio with the VARA FM modem via the local RMS (Radio Message Server) gateway.

Winlink: VHF/UHF VARA FM

Share: USB, physical handoff

C

No Local Gateway,
HF Radio

PACE: C · CONTINGENCY

All local infrastructure destroyed. HF reaches gateways nationwide. Below this: PACE E, the runner.

Winlink: HF (VARA HF)

Share: USB, FRS/GMRS voice relay

Forms, templates and Message Center work the same at every level. This slide says what has failed; the PACE plan (next slide) says what we do

Cell is the first to fail — typically 2–4 hours of battery backup, heavy congestion from the start. Wired internet (fiber/cable backbone) is more resilient. Starlink is very resilient because its orbital signal is unaffected by ground-level infrastructure damage. Amateur radio needs only a battery. Each card carries its PACE level: cell is Primary; any surviving internet, wired or Starlink, is Alternate; VARA FM and then VARA HF radio are Contingency. Below HF there is no electronic path at all; that is Emergency, the runner.
